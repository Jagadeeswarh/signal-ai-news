01CHINA / COMPUTE INFRASTRUCTURENEW6 NOV 2026

China Mobile puts more than ¥100bn behind compute and network expansion

The carrier plans more than ¥100bn in 2027 computing-power investment, with seven gigawatt-class parks and a unified network linking clusters, fibre and edge capacity.

China Mobile plans to spend more than 100 billion yuan on computing power in 2027, within total investment and procurement expected to exceed 500 billion yuan. Chairman Chen Zhongyue made the announcement at the 2026 Mobile Network Conference on November 6. Caixin reports that about one-fifth of next year’s total investment and procurement is intended for data centres.

Chen said China Mobile already has multiple 10,000-card intelligent-computing clusters and expects computing capacity to double in each of the next two years. The company has begun construction on three gigawatt-class computing parks in 2026, with 1.5GW of capacity expected to be delivered, and plans two more in 2027, taking the total to seven. There is a small source discrepancy: Caixin’s summary line reverses the 2026/2027 split, while its article body and C114 both quote the chairman as saying three this year and two next year. The seven-park target is clear; the annual split should be treated cautiously.

The build is not only about server halls. China Mobile says it will expand 400G optical transport networking (OTN), connect national computing hubs, deploy more hollow-core fibre, upgrade content-delivery networks (CDNs) and use unified scheduling to match computing supply with demand.

The announcements do not identify accelerator suppliers, GPU models, high-bandwidth memory configurations or the share of capacity that will use Chinese-designed chips. A large procurement plan is not proof of delivered capacity or a mature domestic software stack.

Evidence check: The figures come from China Mobile chairman Chen Zhongyue’s November 6 conference remarks, reported by Caixin and C114. They are company plans and targets. Caixin’s summary line conflicts with its article body on the year-by-year campus split; the seven-campus total is consistent.

Why developers should care: Regional compute access depends on power, cooling, memory, networking and scheduling as well as accelerators. 400G OTN, hollow-core fibre and unified scheduling can affect cluster utilization and latency. Developers should evaluate actual cloud hardware and network paths rather than headline campus capacity.

Why it ranks here: This is the largest explicit compute-and-network investment signal in the 48-hour window and a major China capacity plan. No chip supplier was named, so it is not evidence that Huawei or another domestic accelerator has won the orders.

Sources: C114 — 6 Nov 2026 · Caixin — 6 Nov 2026 (Chinese)
02CLOUD / GPU ECONOMICSNEW6 NOV 2026

Microsoft winds down Oracle AI-compute rental as older Nvidia GPUs no longer fit

The 2023 agreement is being switched off after Microsoft declined to renew it. Oracle says demand for earlier-generation GPUs remains strong.

Microsoft is turning off AI computing capacity it rented from Oracle after declining to renew a multi-year agreement announced in 2023, Reuters reported on November 6, relaying Bloomberg’s reporting and citing people familiar with the matter. The rented hardware used an older generation of Nvidia GPUs and, according to Bloomberg’s sources, became less valuable as Microsoft’s needs changed.

Oracle told Reuters that demand for OCI AI infrastructure remains strong, including for earlier-generation GPUs. Microsoft did not immediately respond, and the financial terms of the contract were not disclosed. The move is not evidence that AI compute demand has disappeared; it shows how quickly workload requirements can change the value of rented capacity.

Reuters also noted separate delays to IREN deliveries after Microsoft requested network-design changes, and tighter oversight of some neocloud projects. Those are related capacity-management signals, but they are not part of the Oracle contract itself.

Evidence check: Reuters published the report on November 6, citing Bloomberg and people familiar with the matter. Oracle’s response is on the record; Microsoft did not comment. No contract value or exact GPU model was disclosed.

Why developers should care: GPU generation, memory, interconnect and performance per watt can matter more than a headline accelerator count. Teams buying cloud capacity should benchmark the hardware against their actual training and inference workloads and understand upgrade and renewal terms.

Why it ranks here: Ranks second because it exposes hardware-obsolescence and cloud-sourcing risk in a major hyperscaler’s AI stack. It is a separate contract from Microsoft’s Nscale decision covered in the previous issue.

Sources: Reuters, relaying Bloomberg — 6 Nov 2026
03LATIN AMERICA / DATA CENTRESNEW7 NOV 2026

ByteDance weighs a larger second phase for its Brazil data-centre project

Bloomberg reports that the TikTok owner is looking for a developer in Ceará. The possible expansion is larger than the US$10bn, 300MW project already under construction.

ByteDance is considering a second phase of its data-centre project at an industrial port in Ceará, northeastern Brazil, and is seeking a developer to oversee it, Bloomberg News reported on November 7. Sources told Bloomberg Línea that the expansion would be larger than the US$10 billion, 300MW project already under construction at Pecém.

Some sources also described early talks about a similarly sized facility outside the port. The Pecém project benefits from tax incentives linked to exporting data; a separate site outside the port would serve Brazilian customers. ByteDance did not respond to Bloomberg Línea’s request for comment, and no final scale or timetable has been announced.

Brazil’s data-centre capacity has more than doubled since 2022 to above 1GW, according to industry group Brasscom. The country is offering tax incentives for imported servers and network equipment, but grid transmission constraints remain a practical limit on where new capacity can be connected.

Evidence check: Bloomberg Línea published the report on November 7, attributing the proposed second phase and parallel site talks to unnamed sources. ByteDance did not comment. The expansion remains under consideration, not a confirmed investment decision.

Why developers should care: Regional compute location affects latency, data residency, renewable-energy sourcing and access to Latin American customers. Announced megawatts still need power, transmission, cooling, fibre and permits before they become usable capacity.

Why it ranks here: Ranks third because it points to a potentially large non-US infrastructure build and a new regional market. The project is preliminary, so the headline keeps the conditional language.

Sources: Bloomberg Línea / Bloomberg News — 7 Nov 2026
04EUROPE / POLICY & INFRASTRUCTURENEW6 NOV 2026

European data-centre protests reach a Microsoft construction site

Police in Amsterdam detained 150–200 people after a site occupation. The wider campaign called for a pause on large projects in 15 European countries.

Protesters broke into a Microsoft data-centre construction site in Amsterdam on November 6, with some climbing a crane and setting off flares. Dutch police said they detained 150–200 people suspected of trespassing. The Associated Press reports the action came during a wider two-day wave of protests in 15 European countries over energy, water and community-impact concerns.

There is an important distinction between the groups involved. The Guardian reports that the site occupation by Dutch direct-action group Geef Tegengas was separate from the Stop Dirty Data Centres coalition’s campaign actions outside the fence, even though both targeted the same project. The coalition says more than 100 groups took part in its Europe-wide days of action.

Microsoft said it respected peaceful protest but did not condone unlawful or unsafe actions. These demonstrations do not amount to a moratorium or a change in permitting rules, but they show that community consent and local infrastructure can become project risks alongside land, power and financing.

Evidence check: AP reports the police detention figure and the 15-country campaign; the Guardian clarifies that the site occupation was separate from the coalition’s outside-the-fence actions. The coalition’s own website states its demand for a moratorium.

Why developers should care: AI infrastructure roadmaps depend on local approvals, grid connections, water availability and community acceptance. Developers and operators need to account for siting, cooling and energy disclosure before projects are announced as capacity wins.

Why it ranks here: Ranks fourth because opposition has moved from general debate into a visible project-site disruption in a major European market. The report separates the direct action from the coalition’s wider campaign.

Sources: Associated Press — 6 Nov 2026 · The Guardian — 6 Nov 2026 · Stop Dirty Data Centres — campaign statement
05SCIENCE / SUSTAINABILITYNEW7 NOV 2026

Conservation groups adopt AI while trying to limit its footprint

Reuters finds practical gains in wildfire alerts, wildlife surveys and field-note digitisation — alongside concern about the electricity and water behind data centres.

Reuters interviewed 15 environmental, conservation and development organizations about their AI use. All said they use the technology in some form, and all expressed concern about the environmental impact of the data centres that power it. Conservation International uses satellite analysis to alert forest patrols in Cambodia to possible wildfires.

Save the Elephants said AI models running on Nvidia GeForce platforms can analyse about 200,000 aerial images in hours, compared with nine people taking nine months to review them manually. The Jane Goodall Institute is using an AWS tool to scan, translate and structure handwritten chimpanzee field notes from Tanzania; a task once estimated at 13–15 years is now expected to take about two.

Some organizations are changing the way they run models. World Resources Institute caches repeat queries and limits resource-intensive generation. The Nature Conservancy processes fishing-vessel footage on AI cameras aboard the vessels instead of sending every video to central data centres. The International Energy Agency estimates global data centres could use 945TWh of electricity and 1.2 trillion litres of water annually by 2030.

Evidence check: Reuters’ November 7 report is based on interviews with 15 organizations. The use cases are organization-reported; the 2030 energy and water figures are IEA estimates, not measurements of current consumption.

Why developers should care: Caching, batching, model choice and edge inference can change the energy and data-transfer footprint of production AI. Measure workload-level resource use rather than treating renewable-energy purchases as the whole sustainability story.

Why it ranks here: Ranks fifth because it connects real, specific AI benefits to the infrastructure costs that increasingly shape deployment. The reporting includes both use cases and practical mitigation techniques.

Sources: Reuters — 7 Nov 2026

AI in context

Analysis · not reported fact

1. Compute is becoming a regional policy choice

China Mobile’s investment plan and ByteDance’s Brazil expansion point to a wider geography for AI infrastructure. The next competition is about power, transmission, cooling, fibre, tax rules and data residency as much as GPU supply. Neither announcement yet identifies the accelerator mix or proves that the planned capacity will arrive on schedule.

2. Hardware age is now part of cloud economics

Microsoft’s Oracle wind-down is a concrete example of rented capacity losing value as the workload changes. Older GPUs may still serve other customers, as Oracle says, but a hyperscaler’s frontier workloads can outgrow a contract before the facility is physically old. Performance per watt, memory and interconnect are becoming contract-level issues.

3. AI’s social licence will be built locally

The European protests and Reuters’ conservation reporting show both sides of the same infrastructure question. AI can help count wildlife and detect fires, while the data centres behind it draw electricity and water. Caching, edge inference and smaller models can reduce some costs; public consultation and transparent resource accounting determine whether new sites can be built.

4. Embodied AI has a different data bottleneck

The Caixin interview series describes three complementary training sources: internet video for broad physical priors, simulation for cheap scalable pretraining, and real-robot interaction for deployment. Its newest instalment adds a possible industry layer: specialist data-collection centres. The practical questions are data diversity, rights, annotation quality, compute cost and whether learned skills transfer to unstructured tasks.

Plain-English glossary

Terms used in this edition
GW-scale campus
A data-centre development designed around gigawatt-class power capacity; the phrase does not mean the full amount is already connected or operating.
10,000-card cluster
A large accelerator cluster with roughly ten thousand cards; the term does not specify chip model, interconnect or usable throughput.
AI compute infrastructure
The combined power, cooling, networking, accelerators, memory and software needed to run AI workloads.
GPU generation
A hardware family or architecture generation. Newer generations can improve throughput or efficiency, but workload fit must be measured.
Cloud capacity rental
Leasing another provider’s servers or accelerator capacity instead of owning and operating the hardware.
Data residency
Rules or business requirements governing where data is stored and processed.
Edge inference
Running a model near the source of data, such as on a camera or local device, instead of sending all inputs to a central cloud.
Observational data
Records of what happened in real-world practice, rather than data from a controlled experiment assigning interventions.
Identifiability
Whether the available data and assumptions are sufficient to distinguish the underlying causal or physiological process.
Treatment-policy confounding
When a model learns clinicians’ past choices from records and mistakes those choices for the biological effect of treatment.
Agent harness
The prompts, tools, control loop and execution environment that govern an AI agent’s actions.
Bug bounty
A program that authorizes researchers to report security vulnerabilities under defined rules and scope.
OTN
Optical transport network: high-capacity fibre infrastructure that moves data between data centres and network hubs.
CDN
Content-delivery network: distributed servers that bring content and services closer to users.
Sim-to-real
The process of transferring a policy or skill trained in simulation to a physical robot.
Embodied AI
AI systems that perceive and act in the physical world, including robots and autonomous machines.
