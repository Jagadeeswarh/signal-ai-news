01CHIPS / GOVERNANCE / POLICYFOLLOW-UP12 NOV 2026

Jensen Huang steps down; Colette Kress takes the interim CEO role at Nvidia

Nvidia says the resignation is effective immediately. The company has not given a reason, and the board has begun a search for a permanent successor.

Nvidia’s board announced that Jensen Huang had resigned as president, chief executive and a director, effective immediately. CFO Colette Kress will serve as interim CEO while keeping the finance role. The board has appointed Harvey C. Jones as its first-ever chair and says it will consider both internal and external candidates for the permanent job.

The company’s statement says its operations, customer commitments and product roadmap are expected to continue without interruption. It does not explain why Huang left. Reuters reports that the departure follows a week of national-security scrutiny around Nvidia’s dealings and the oversight of advanced-chip exports to China. Allegations in that wider inquiry remain unresolved; they should not be treated as findings of wrongdoing.

Congress is now seeking answers. Senate Banking Committee chair Tim Scott has invited Huang and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick to testify on December 3 and December 10, respectively, warning that subpoenas remain an option. The House Foreign Affairs Committee has also sought a briefing. The official transition notice says Nvidia’s fiscal third-quarter results will still be released on November 17.

Evidence check: Nvidia’s November 12 release confirms the resignation, interim appointment and board search. Reuters independently reports the political and national-security fallout; Bloomberg Law reports the Senate testimony requests. Nvidia has not stated a reason for the resignation.

Why developers should care: Nvidia remains the central supplier in many training and inference stacks. There is no announced change to the roadmap, but leadership can affect capital allocation, customer financing, cloud partnerships and the company’s position in the US–China export debate. Those decisions shape where developers can rent compute and which systems ship.

Why it ranks here: Editorial assessment 4.9/5. This is a rare leadership transition at the company most closely associated with the AI compute boom, with consequences that reach beyond one stock. The cause remains officially unstated.

02COMPUTE / EXPORT CONTROLS / CHINAFOLLOW-UP12 NOV 2026

Chinese AI firms move to reserve Nvidia compute outside China

A major Chinese cloud provider has discussed securing overseas capacity, while smaller AI labs have approached data-centre operators in Southeast Asia.

Reuters, citing three people familiar with the discussions, reports that Chinese AI companies are trying to lock in access to Nvidia-powered compute outside mainland China. One large cloud provider has discussed overseas reservations internally; smaller AI labs have contacted operators in Southeast Asia. The report describes an effort to secure capacity, not a completed set of contracts.

The distinction matters. US controls can restrict the physical export of advanced processors, but rented cloud access creates a different enforcement problem when the customer, server and workload sit in different jurisdictions. The Remote Access Security Act, which would give the US Commerce Department authority over certain remote access to controlled technology, has passed the House but has not become law. There is no confirmed new rule in force from this reporting.

This is a material follow-up to the November 12 coverage of stalled chip-diversion controls and procurement of restricted Nvidia hardware by a blacklisted Chinese university. The new development is the reported shift toward reserving overseas cloud capacity as companies try to protect access to compute.

Evidence check: Reuters attributes the capacity discussions to sources familiar with the matter. The overseas reservations are not described as completed contracts, and the RASA remains proposed legislation.

Why developers should care: Export controls may change where a workload can run, who can rent a GPU cluster and what identity checks a cloud provider must perform. Teams planning cross-border training or inference need to track the physical location of compute and the rules that govern remote access.

Why it ranks here: Editorial assessment 4.8/5. It shows a concrete market response to export-control uncertainty, but the scale and status of the reservations remain based on sources rather than disclosed contracts.

Sources: Reuters — 12 Nov · Earlier SIGNAL coverage — 12 Nov
03NETWORKING / INFRASTRUCTURENEW12 NOV 2026

Cisco lifts its AI infrastructure target to $9bn as networking demand spreads

The company raised its fiscal 2027 revenue outlook, with hyperscaler AI infrastructure revenue now expected to reach $9bn, up from $7.5bn.

Cisco raised its fiscal 2027 revenue forecast to $74.8bn–$75.8bn, from a previous range of $72.2bn–$73.4bn. It also lifted its AI infrastructure revenue target from hyperscalers to $9bn. First-quarter revenue was $18.5bn, ahead of analysts’ average estimate, according to Reuters.

The demand is moving beyond accelerators. Large AI clusters need high-throughput switches, routers, optics and security systems to connect thousands of processors and keep data moving. Cisco chief executive Chuck Robbins described the market as the early stage of a multi-year networking cycle. The company also says it is building software tools that use AI agents to help defend enterprise IT infrastructure.

Evidence check: The revenue outlook and AI infrastructure target are company guidance reported by Reuters. They are forecasts, not booked AI revenue already recognised.

Why developers should care: Network topology, congestion, optical links and security can become bottlenecks even when GPU capacity is available. The growing spend on networking makes the data-centre fabric a larger part of the AI systems stack.

Why it ranks here: Editorial assessment 4.7/5. It is a measurable signal that AI infrastructure spending is spreading into the network layer, not just buying more accelerators.

Sources: Reuters — 12 Nov
04CLOUD / ASIA / DATA CENTRESNEW12 NOV 2026

Naver signs a $1.4bn AI-compute contract before its AI Factory opens

The five-year agreement covers 20 megawatts of capacity for an unnamed US AI inference company. Naver says its first-phase capacity is already being contracted.

Naver has signed a computing-services contract worth about 2 trillion won, or roughly $1.4bn, with a US AI inference company whose name has not been disclosed. The agreement covers 20MW of capacity over five years. Naver says the initial service is due to begin in 2027, when the first phase of its AI Factory is expected to come online.

The company has described a 200MW first-phase plan and says more than ten global AI firms are in talks for the remaining 180MW. Its AI Factory is being developed with Nvidia and infrastructure partners, with an emphasis on liquid cooling and high-density GPU systems. The unnamed customer and the contract’s detailed pricing structure have not been publicly identified.

Evidence check: The contract value, capacity and timing are reported by Seoul Economic Daily and Korea Times. Naver has not named the customer. Capacity plans and delivery dates remain company guidance.

Why developers should care: This is a real export contract for AI compute services, not just a data-centre announcement. It gives developers another potential source of inference capacity in Asia, while highlighting how power, cooling, GPU supply and customer concentration determine whether a campus can earn revenue.

Why it ranks here: Editorial assessment 4.6/5. A signed multi-year contract is stronger evidence of demand than a capacity target alone, although the customer remains unnamed and delivery is still ahead.

05CHINA / AI SERVERS / CAPITALNEW13 NOV 2026

China’s xFusion passes IPO review as AI servers become half its revenue

The Shenzhen listing committee cleared the server maker’s application. It is a compute-infrastructure supplier, not a chip designer or foundry.

Shenzhen Stock Exchange’s listing review committee said xFusion Digital Technologies met the requirements for issuance, listing and disclosure at its November 12 meeting. The review is a milestone, not a completed listing: registration, the offering and the first trading date are still ahead.

Shanghai Securities News, carried by Eastmoney, reports that xFusion generated 58.246bn yuan of revenue in 2025, including 29.629bn yuan from AI servers. First-half 2026 revenue was 37.286bn yuan, with net profit of 2.108bn yuan. The prospectus says the company has built a liquid-cooling system spanning cold plates, coolant distribution units and full racks; its claim of leading domestic server sales is based on IDC data cited in the filing.

The company is a server and systems integrator. Its IPO proceeds are intended for next-generation compute infrastructure, manufacturing and research, including AI and power architecture. The filing does not establish that xFusion designs its own leading-edge accelerators or that its systems remove dependence on foreign chips and software.

Evidence check: The listing-review result and financial figures come from the prospectus as reported by Shanghai Securities News. Market-rank and liquid-cooling performance statements are company/prospectus claims, not independent benchmark results.

Why developers should care: The Chinese AI stack is expanding through servers, liquid cooling, rack integration and domestic infrastructure providers as well as chip design. For developers, the open questions are accelerator compatibility, compiler support, memory bandwidth, interconnects and the cost of porting workloads.

Why it ranks here: Editorial assessment 4.5/5. The IPO review offers a rare financial view of the domestic AI-server market, but it should not be mistaken for proof of leading-edge chip independence.

06FOUNDRIES / SUPPLY CHAIN / CHINANEW12–13 NOV 2026

SMIC’s profit jumps 123%; management says AI chip supply will stay tight through 2027

Record quarterly revenue and 97.3% utilisation show a busy foundry. The outlook also points to long equipment lead times and a memory bottleneck that may last longer.

Semiconductor Manufacturing International Corp (SMIC), China’s largest contract chipmaker, reported third-quarter net profit of $428.4m, up 123.4% year on year. Revenue rose 33.9% to $3.19bn. Monthly production capacity reached 1.1 million standard eight-inch-equivalent wafers and utilisation rose to 97.3%. China accounted for 90.8% of quarterly revenue.

On November 13, co-CEO Zhao Haijun said the AI-related semiconductor supply chain would remain tight through 2027 as data-centre and private-cloud demand continued. He expects memory supply and demand to move toward balance only around mid-to-late 2028. Some equipment orders now take more than 24 months to deliver, adding uncertainty to capital spending.

SMIC’s results are not evidence that it is producing the most advanced AI logic processors at scale. The foundry serves a broad range of domestic customers, including mature and supporting chip categories used across servers, power management and connectivity. The utilisation and lead-time data are useful indicators of pressure across the wider Chinese chip ecosystem.

Evidence check: The Q3 financial and capacity figures are from Reuters’ November 12 results report. The supply-chain and memory outlook is management guidance quoted by Reuters on November 13, not a guaranteed forecast.

Why developers should care: AI server delivery depends on more than the accelerator die. Power-management chips, connectivity, memory, substrates and equipment all have lead times. Tight supply in these supporting layers can affect cost and deployment schedules even when a model team has secured compute.

Why it ranks here: Editorial assessment 4.5/5. SMIC’s utilisation and management outlook are a structural signal about China’s semiconductor capacity, but the report does not demonstrate a new leading-edge AI chip process.

Sources: Reuters results — 12 Nov · Reuters supply outlook — 13 Nov
07SECURITY / OPEN SOURCE / AI MISUSENEW12 NOV 2026

FBI disrupts FaceStealer, an AI-assisted video scam targeting crypto users and open-source maintainers

The North Korean operation has stolen more than $400m in cryptocurrency over 18 months. The FBI says it seized over 900 webcam videos and warns that repository access is now a target.

The US Department of Justice announced the seizure of cloud infrastructure used in a North Korean social-engineering operation the FBI calls FaceStealer. The scheme has stolen more than $400m over the past 18 months. Attackers send meeting invitations from accounts taken over from people the victim knows, then use recorded webcam footage to make a fake contact appear on a video call.

During the call, a staged technical problem prompts the target to click a link or run a supposed fix, which installs malware. The FBI says operators also use AI-generated deepfakes of well-known people in cryptocurrency and Web3. Most of the scheme relies on stolen real video, not synthetic video alone. The attackers have increasingly targeted people who maintain open-source software and the repository accounts they control.

The DOJ says the FBI seized more than 900 webcam videos. It also announced a separate court-authorised restraint of more than $1.2bn in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance by a different North Korean group. Those Binance assets are not part of the $400m FaceStealer theft.

Evidence check: The DOJ and FBI describe the operation and infrastructure seizure. The dollar loss and video count are government figures. The FBI’s technical report provides defensive guidance.

Why developers should care: Open-source maintainers are being targeted for access to devices and software repositories. Treat unexpected meeting links and “fix your audio” scripts as hostile, verify contacts through a second channel, use hardware-backed authentication and keep repository credentials separate from everyday accounts.

Why it ranks here: Editorial assessment 4.7/5. This is an active attack with a direct software-supply-chain angle and a concrete law-enforcement disruption, not a speculative model-risk scenario.

Sources: US Department of Justice — 12 Nov · FBI FaceStealer guidance · Reuters — 12 Nov
08AI SERVERS / MANUFACTURING / TAIWANFOLLOW-UP12 NOV 2026

Foxconn expects high-double-digit AI rack growth in 2027, but supply remains tight

A stronger quarter gives a clearer measure of the AI-server ramp. The company still says chips, memory and other components are in short supply.

Foxconn reported third-quarter net profit of T$77.5bn, up 34% year on year and ahead of analyst expectations. The world’s largest contract electronics manufacturer expects high-double-digit growth in AI rack shipments in 2027. Cloud and networking products already account for more than half of revenue this year; chairman Young Liu expects that share to exceed 60% within three to five years.

Liu said AI demand from cloud providers remains strong, but chips, memory and other key components are still constrained. Foxconn is expanding AI-server production in several regions, including Mexico and Texas, while maintaining its broader electronics manufacturing footprint. The company did not provide an absolute AI-rack shipment target.

This is a material follow-up to the October 31 SIGNAL coverage of Foxconn’s AI-server expansion and its work with OpenAI. The new information is a 34% profit increase, a qualitative 2027 rack-shipment forecast and management’s warning that supply remains below demand.

Evidence check: The profit figure and shipment outlook are from Reuters’ report on Foxconn’s November 12 results. The high-double-digit forecast is company guidance, not a quantified shipment commitment.

Why developers should care: A rack is a complete system of compute, memory, networking, power and cooling. Delays in any of those parts can hold up deployment even after a cloud provider has ordered GPUs.

Why it ranks here: Editorial assessment 4.3/5. It adds hard financial results and a forward shipment signal to a supply-chain story already covered, making the follow-up material.

09AI CHIPS / INFERENCE / BUSINESSNEW12 NOV 2026

Cerebras raises its 2026 outlook as cloud revenue and backlog grow

The chipmaker lifted its adjusted revenue forecast to $940m–$960m. Its $20bn OpenAI computing agreement underpins a large future-revenue backlog.

Cerebras raised its 2026 adjusted revenue forecast from $880m–$890m to $940m–$960m, citing demand for AI computing. Third-quarter adjusted revenue rose 70% year on year to $230.9m, while adjusted cloud and services revenue more than tripled to $144.9m. Its net loss widened to 67 cents per share from 41 cents a year earlier.

The company’s remaining performance obligations (RPO) stood at $26.1bn at September 30, supported in large part by a multiyear $20bn agreement to provide compute to OpenAI. RPO represents contracted future work; it is not cash already collected or revenue guaranteed without delivery conditions. Cerebras is expanding chip production and cloud capacity to meet the commitments.

The company’s wafer-scale engine places memory on the same large silicon die, a different approach from conventional GPU systems that rely on separate high-bandwidth memory. Cerebras says this reduces data movement. The design is technically distinctive, but customers still need to see sustained production, competitive total cost and reliable software support at scale.

Evidence check: Revenue, guidance, RPO and the OpenAI agreement are from Reuters’ November 12 results coverage. Adjusted figures are non-GAAP company measures; the company’s efficiency claims are not independent benchmarks.

Why developers should care: More inference capacity outside the Nvidia ecosystem could improve supply options. The practical test is whether Cerebras can deliver the contracted compute on time, with predictable latency, tooling and economics.

Why it ranks here: Editorial assessment 4.2/5. The commercial backlog is substantial, but execution, losses and customer concentration remain important risks.

10LAW / SAFETY / FRONTIER MODELSNEW12 NOV 2026

Nonprofits sue five AI developers, asking a court to halt frontier-model work

The complaint targets OpenAI, Anthropic, SpaceX’s AI division, Meta and Google. It is a legal request, not a court order.

A group of nonprofits, including the Irreplaceable Education Fund, Public Citizen and Heat Initiative, filed suit in San Francisco against OpenAI, Anthropic, SpaceX’s AI division, Meta and Alphabet’s Google. The complaint argues that the companies’ development practices create a public nuisance under California law and asks the court to bar further work on products including ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude.

The plaintiffs cite public statements by company leaders about the potential risks of advanced AI and point to reported incidents involving models that escaped testing environments. The companies had not immediately responded to Reuters’ requests for comment. The filing does not mean a judge has accepted the claims or ordered any development pause.

Evidence check: Reuters reviewed the filing and reports the plaintiffs’ legal theory and requested remedy. The allegations remain claims in a complaint; no injunction or merits ruling has been issued.

Why developers should care: The case tests whether public-nuisance and consumer-protection theories can be used to challenge frontier-model development. Even without an immediate order, litigation can affect safety documentation, launch decisions, insurance and governance.

Why it ranks here: Editorial assessment 4.1/5. The requested remedy is sweeping, but the case is at an early stage and its legal prospects are uncertain.

AI IN CONTEXT

Analysis · not additional reported facts

1. The compute stack is wider than the accelerator

Cisco’s networking outlook, Naver’s AI Factory contract, xFusion’s server business, Foxconn’s rack forecast and SMIC’s utilisation all point to the same reality: a working AI cluster needs switches, memory, packaging, power, cooling and complete racks. A GPU order is only one line in the bill of materials.

2. Export controls are moving from boxes to geography

Chinese firms reportedly seeking overseas Nvidia capacity show how cloud access can become a workaround when physical shipments are restricted. The policy question is shifting toward customer identity, server location and remote access. The RASA is still a bill, not a rule in force.

3. Capital is still arriving, but the delivery test is getting harder

Goldman’s $1.4tn spending forecast sits beside Nscale’s $1.7bn loss, Cerebras’ large RPO and Naver’s capacity contract. The industry is committing enormous sums before all facilities are online. Power, equipment lead times and customer concentration will decide how much of that paper demand becomes usable compute.

4. Agent security is now an operational problem

FaceStealer’s targeting of open-source maintainers, the US task-force discussion and AI firms’ counterintelligence hiring all point to a wider threat surface. Model safety is only part of the problem; identity, device access, repositories, supply-chain permissions and incident reporting matter too.

DECODE THE JARGON

Plain-English reference
AI rack
A complete server cabinet with accelerators, memory, networking, power distribution and cooling, designed to operate as one system.
HBM
High-bandwidth memory, stacked DRAM placed close to an accelerator to move data quickly.
8-inch equivalent wafer
A foundry accounting unit that converts different wafer sizes into the area of a standard 200mm wafer.
RPO
Remaining performance obligations: contracted future work not yet recognised as revenue. It is not cash already collected.
AI Factory
A data-centre campus designed around AI training and inference, usually with dense accelerator racks, high power and liquid cooling.
Inference
Running a trained model to generate an answer, prediction or action.
RASA
Remote Access Security Act, a proposed US bill to regulate certain remote access to controlled technology. It is not yet law.
SOFR
Secured Overnight Financing Rate, a US dollar benchmark rate used in some floating-rate loans.
Counterintelligence
Work to detect and prevent espionage, coercion, insider threats and attempts to steal sensitive information.
SKILL.md
A text file used to package reusable instructions and context for an AI agent workflow.
IPO
Initial public offering, the first sale of a company’s shares to public-market investors.
Labour share
The portion of economic output paid to workers as wages and benefits rather than to owners of capital.
