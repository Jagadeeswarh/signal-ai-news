01RESEARCH / SOFTWARE SECURITYNEW10 NOV 2026

DeepMind’s agents formally verify key Signal code, down to the machine instructions

AlphaProof Code produced about 6.5 million lines of machine-checked Lean proofs. The result is substantial, but its scope is narrower than “the whole Signal app is proven secure.”

Google DeepMind says its new AlphaProof Code framework has proved key security properties of libsignal, the reference implementation of the Signal protocol, at the compiled machine-code level. The work covers the post-quantum PQXDH handshake and Triple Ratchet, including forward secrecy, authentication and post-compromise security. The team reports about 6.5 million lines of Lean proof code, checked by Lean’s kernel, built around roughly 26,000 lines of trusted specifications and AArch64 machine-code semantics.

The workflow is a hybrid. Human researchers choose and review the properties that should hold; AI agents draft and refine the formal specification, then separate prover agents try to establish that the compiled code satisfies it. A gatekeeper accepts a proof only when Lean checks it against the approved specification. DeepMind says the campaign took four weeks and typically used teams of 40–80 agents on one machine for 24–72 hours.

The limits matter. The paper analyses libsignal v0.105.4 in one-to-one sessions; group messaging is future work. The model does not cover threads, signals, dynamic linking or files outside the current directory, and the proof relies on stated assumptions about cryptographic primitives and some external routines. It is a proof of specified properties under a defined execution model, not a blanket certification of every Signal client or deployment.

Evidence check: Google DeepMind published both the research explanation and a 57-page technical paper on 10 November. Signal CTO Ehren Kret is quoted in the announcement welcoming the expanded verification. The scope and assumptions above come from the paper itself, not the headline claim.

Why developers should care: The interesting change is the economics of formal assurance. Agents can now do much of the proof-writing once humans have defined what must be true; a small trusted kernel checks their work. That could make machine-checked guarantees more practical for cryptography, firmware and infrastructure code, while leaving specification design and review firmly in human hands.

Why it ranks here: Editorial assessment 4.9/5. It is a concrete demonstration of AI moving beyond code generation into machine-checked assurance of production software, with unusually clear technical documentation and explicit limits.

TSMC backs Anthropic and OpenAI as it authorises another US$40.9bn for capacity

The world’s leading contract chipmaker is becoming a direct investor in two major model customers while expanding advanced-node, packaging and fab capacity.

TSMC’s board approved investments of up to US$500m in Anthropic at its IPO price and up to US$500m in OpenAI preferred stock. It also authorised up to US$500m to establish an investment company for strategic investments across the semiconductor industry and related ecosystem.

In the same set of resolutions, the board approved about US$40.927bn in capital appropriations. TSMC says the money is mainly for installing and upgrading advanced-process capacity, advanced packaging, mature and specialty technologies, fab construction and facility systems, plus 2027 research and sustaining capital. This is an authorisation, not a statement that the full amount will be spent immediately.

The Anthropic prospectus filed the same day separately lists TSMC Partners among buyers in a US$26bn private placement alongside AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft and Nvidia. Reuters reports the combined public offer and private placement could raise up to US$90.4bn. These are proposed investments and financing commitments, subject to the offering and transaction conditions; they are not chip purchase orders.

Evidence check: TSMC’s board-resolution release is the primary source for the two US$500m authorisations and US$40.9bn capital budget. Anthropic’s SEC filing identifies the strategic placement participants. Reuters reports the broader financing totals.

Why developers should care: The model companies need more accelerators, packaging and power; the foundry supplying much of that capacity is also taking equity exposure to its customers. That can align long-term planning, but it also tightens the loop between infrastructure suppliers, model revenue and financing. Watch actual capacity additions and customer demand, not just board approvals.

Why it ranks here: Editorial assessment 4.8/5. This links the AI model race directly to semiconductor capital allocation and advanced packaging, with consequences for capacity, financing and supply-chain concentration.

03POWER / INFRASTRUCTURE FINANCENEW10 NOV 2026

SoftBank books a US$3.7bn loss after revising US AI data-centre power-equipment plans

The company reserved manufacturing capacity and paid suppliers ahead of expected shortages. Project requirements changed; part of those advances could not be recovered.

SoftBank Group’s six-month results show a net loss attributable to shareholders of ¥768bn, compared with a ¥2.924tn profit in the same period a year earlier. Among several factors, the company separately recognised a ¥597.2bn (US$3.7bn) loss tied to revised procurement plans for power-generation equipment for AI data-centre projects in the United States.

The investor presentation says SoftBank subsidiaries had paid US$9.5bn since FY2025 under manufacturing-capacity reservations, equipment purchase contracts and related arrangements intended to secure supply ahead of shortages. Of that, US$2.9bn was non-refundable advance payments for equipment no longer required under revised plans, and US$0.9bn related to contract modifications and settlements. SoftBank says it may receive rebates of up to US$2.1bn on future equipment purchases, but those rights are conditional and no asset has been recognised.

This is a warning about the physical side of the AI build-out. Power generation equipment has long lead times, and hyperscale projects can change their load, location or delivery schedule before the hardware arrives. Reserving scarce capacity can protect a project; it can also leave expensive deposits stranded when the plan changes.

Evidence check: SoftBank’s 10 November Q2 FY2026 presentation and consolidated financial report disclose the six-month loss and procurement charge. The US$2.1bn rebates are potential future rights subject to conditions, not cash already recovered.

Why developers should care: Compute roadmaps depend on power equipment, substations, turbines, transformers and grid connections as much as on GPUs. Procurement slippage can delay clusters and change the economics of reserved capacity. This is a real cost of building ahead of demand.

Why it ranks here: Editorial assessment 4.7/5. The charge is unusually concrete evidence of the risks in AI infrastructure procurement and the mismatch between fast-moving project plans and slow industrial supply chains.

Infineon expects data-centre power-supply revenue to approach €4bn next year

The German chipmaker finished FY2026 with a record quarter and expects more than 20% revenue growth in FY2027, with data-centre power a major driver.

Infineon reported fourth-quarter FY2026 revenue of €4.706bn and full-year revenue of €16.353bn, up 12% year on year. For FY2027 it forecasts revenue of about €20bn and says sales from power supplies for data centres should come close to €4bn. The company plans €3.0bn–€3.5bn in investment next year.

These products sit below the accelerator itself: power-management devices and conversion systems help move electricity from the grid and server rack down to the processor’s voltage rails. As AI systems draw more power and concentrate it into denser racks, efficiency, thermal limits and transient response become important design constraints. Infineon’s forecast is a company projection, not an independent market-size estimate.

Evidence check: Infineon’s FY2026 results release, dated 10 November, reports the record quarter, revenue forecast and data-centre power outlook. The €4bn figure is a forecast for FY2027.

Why developers should care: Hardware teams designing AI servers need to account for power delivery, not just accelerator TDP. Better power conversion can improve rack density and operating cost, while supply of power semiconductors becomes another constraint to track alongside HBM and advanced packaging.

Why it ranks here: Editorial assessment 4.6/5. The forecast quantifies how much the AI data-centre build-out is changing the power-semiconductor business, a less visible but essential layer of compute supply.

05AI CLOUD / BUSINESS ECONOMICSNEW10 NOV 2026

CoreWeave’s backlog hits US$132.5bn as quarterly revenue climbs to US$3.66bn

The AI cloud provider is adding power and infrastructure at speed. Its latest filing also shows a US$709m quarterly net loss and US$846m in net interest expense.

CoreWeave reported third-quarter revenue of US$3.661bn, up from US$1.365bn a year earlier. Its reported revenue backlog reached US$132.5bn at 30 September, and active power capacity rose above 1.7GW. Contracted power exceeded 4.6GW, including more than 1.3GW across India, Indonesia and Spain.

The growth comes with a heavy capital bill. CoreWeave reported a US$709m quarterly net loss and US$846m in net interest expense. It raised its full-year 2026 capital-expenditure outlook to US$37bn–US$39bn, expects about US$9bn in customer prepayments and deposits over the next three months, and raised its revenue guidance to US$13.0bn–US$13.3bn. The backlog is not the same as guaranteed cash: the company says delivery and service-availability conditions still apply.

CoreWeave also launched Forge, an AI development environment that brings training, inference, evaluation and agent-development workflows together. That is a product move beyond renting GPU capacity, but the release does not provide independent evidence that Forge changes the economics of the underlying infrastructure business.

Evidence check: The figures and Forge launch are in CoreWeave’s 10 November earnings exhibit filed with the SEC. The company’s non-GAAP measures and forward guidance should be read alongside its GAAP net loss, interest costs and debt disclosures.

Why developers should care: The provider’s India and Southeast Asia power footprint could affect where teams can place workloads and meet data-residency requirements. For engineering leaders, compare available capacity, network latency and total cost against the financial durability of the provider, not only GPU availability.

Why it ranks here: Editorial assessment 4.5/5. CoreWeave is a useful live case study of AI-cloud demand, power procurement, debt and customer prepayments converging in one business.

06AI SAFETY / RESEARCH OPERATIONSNEW9 NOV 2026

OpenAI moves its research kill switch into the inference layer

Control Room checks a job ID before an inference engine returns output. OpenAI says a stop instruction takes effect within 35 seconds at most, and the system fails closed if it goes down.

OpenAI has deployed Control Room across its research infrastructure. Each research workload that uses agentic inference must register with an approved safety case and receive a job ID. When the workload asks an inference engine for model output, the engine checks with Control Room; if the job has been stopped, the request is denied.

The key design choice is where the enforcement sits. OpenAI does not rely on the experiment’s own code to notice that it has been stopped. Instead, the check is placed in the inference layer, which the company says has fewer and more centralised code paths. The service is designed to fail closed: if Control Room is unavailable or tampered with, covered engines deny inference requests until it recovers.

There is a latency caveat. OpenAI says the maximum time between receiving a stop instruction and making further progress impossible is 35 seconds, due to cached inference authorisations. The clock starts after a human or monitoring system has decided to intervene; it does not measure the time needed to detect suspicious behaviour. OpenAI says it is working to reduce the delay to zero. The system is internal research infrastructure, not a public API feature.

Evidence check: OpenAI’s technical post, published 9 November, describes the architecture, rollout and 35-second bound. These are company-reported controls; no independent audit or external performance test was included.

Why developers should care: Agent platforms should put revocation at a central model gateway, attach a durable job ID to every request and deny new inference when the policy service is unavailable. A process that remains alive after a stop is not necessarily contained; tool credentials, network calls and queued actions need their own cancellation path.

Why it ranks here: Editorial assessment 4.4/5. This is a concrete operational control for long-running agent workloads, with a measurable stop bound and a clear failure mode.

Sources: OpenAI — Control Room, 9 Nov · Gadgets 360 — 10 Nov
07CODING AGENTS / MODEL AVAILABILITYFOLLOW-UP10 NOV 2026

Google opens Gemini 4 Carbon to Ultra users and previews Antigravity’s /swarm teams

The earlier restricted model is now available to Google AI Ultra subscribers. A new command coordinates parallel workers and a verifier inside Antigravity.

Google Antigravity’s 10 November update makes Gemini 4 Carbon available to Google AI Ultra subscribers in the Antigravity app, CLI and IDE extensions, with enterprise access through Gemini for business. The model was previously introduced with restricted access for selected high-risk research and cybersecurity work. This is a meaningful availability change, not a new model generation.

The new feature is /swarm, now in preview across the Antigravity app and CLI. A coordinator breaks a task into parallel work, assigns workers to isolated Git worktrees so edits do not collide, and can add a verifier to inspect diffs and run tests. Agents communicate on a shared message board with a live roster and graph view. Google says idle workers wait without using tokens, but warns that multi-agent runs consume quota much faster than a single conversation.

The design is more explicit than simply spawning subagents and collecting their answers. Work ownership, verification and the activity trail are visible. The hard questions are whether parallel work reduces total task time after review, whether shared context remains coherent, and how much extra model usage the verifier and coordination loop consume.

Earlier SIGNAL coverage: 29 Oct edition — Gemini 4 Carbon’s restricted launch. What changed: wider paid availability plus a native multi-agent orchestration preview in Antigravity.

Evidence check: Google’s Antigravity blog and documentation describe the release and /swarm workflow. Access tiers and quota behaviour are Google’s own product terms; independent task-completion benchmarks are not yet available.

Why developers should care: This is a practical test of whether agent teams can split repo-scale work without creating a review nightmare. Use isolated worktrees, explicit file ownership, tests and an independent verifier; measure wall-clock time and total token cost against a single-agent baseline.

Why it ranks here: Editorial assessment 4.3/5. The follow-up changes who can use Carbon and adds a real parallel-work surface, but the cost and quality of multi-agent execution still need independent measurement.

Anthropic turns Claude into a shared work surface for spreadsheets, dashboards and connected apps

New artifacts and interactive connectors move Claude from summarising external tools to working inside embedded app interfaces, with MCP as the extension layer.

Anthropic introduced Claude Sheets, Claude Whiteboards and Claude Data, alongside interactive connectors that let partner apps open inside Claude. Sheets supports collaborative spreadsheets and formulas; Whiteboards gives teams a shared space for organising ideas; Claude Data connects to sources such as Snowflake and Salesforce to create dashboards and visual data stories. The Data product is in beta.

Interactive connectors build on the Model Context Protocol (MCP), the open standard Anthropic created for connecting models to tools. Partners can embed their app UI directly in Claude so a user and the assistant can work in the same screen. Anthropic lists Adobe and Atlassian among the embedded apps, and says plugins from Salesforce, NetSuite and Workday include built-in skills, with some still in beta.

Anthropic says connectors operate within existing user permissions and administrators can restrict what an integration may do, such as allowing reads while blocking messages or writes. The company also says Enterprise cloud sessions run in isolated virtual machines. Claude Meetings is described as coming soon; it is not presented as generally available in the announcement.

Evidence check: Anthropic’s product update is dated 10 November. The connector catalogue and permission controls are company descriptions; actual access depends on partner availability, account tier and administrator settings.

Why developers should care: MCP is evolving from a way to expose tools into a way to embed interactive experiences. That raises new design questions around OAuth scopes, write permissions, tenant isolation, audit trails and whether an agent can safely act inside a UI it can also inspect.

Why it ranks here: Editorial assessment 4.2/5. It is a meaningful shift in enterprise agent interfaces and MCP integration, although the product rollout and partner support remain uneven.

Washington and Beijing plan a new AI-risk dialogue alongside next week’s summit

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent says the Shenzhen meeting will cover advanced-model testing and a direct channel for alerting each other to serious incidents.

Bessent said the United States and China should cooperate on shared AI risks while remaining clear-eyed about their strategic differences. This is risk-management diplomacy, not a semiconductor trade agreement. It sits alongside an increasingly tense competition over advanced chips, models and export controls. Whether the channel survives political pressure will matter more than the language of the announcement.

Evidence check: Reuters reported Bessent’s 10 November remarks on 11 November. The meeting and proposed workstreams are described by one US official; there is no published joint communiqué from Beijing confirming operational details.

Why developers should care: Cross-border incident reporting and comparable model evaluations could shape future safety expectations for labs and cloud providers. Developers should watch whether the channel leads to common incident categories, disclosure timelines or testing standards.

10ENERGY / DATA-CENTRE ECONOMICSNEW10 NOV 2026

EIA sees US electricity consumption setting records again in 2026 and 2027

The agency attributes much of the increase to AI data centres and wider electrification. Its forecast puts the grid constraint on a measurable path.

The US Energy Information Administration’s November Short-Term Energy Outlook projects electricity consumption rising from 4,216 billion kilowatt-hours in 2025 to 4,306 billion in 2026 and 4,370 billion in 2027. If the forecast holds, 2026 will be the third consecutive record year. The agency points to AI data centres, along with homes and businesses shifting heating and transport toward electricity.

The EIA expects commercial-sector electricity sales to reach 1,557 billion kWh in 2026, above the previous record. It forecasts renewables’ share of generation rising to 28% in 2027, while coal’s share falls to 15%. These are national forecasts; they do not say how much of the incremental load will come from AI alone, or where new capacity will be connected.

The forecast follows a string of local grid constraints and large data-centre power reservations. It reinforces the point that AI compute growth depends on generation, transmission, transformers, switchgear, cooling and permits. A new accelerator shipment does not translate directly into usable capacity if the site cannot get enough power.

Evidence check: EIA published its November outlook on 10 November. Reuters reports the headline electricity-consumption figures and the agency’s attribution to data centres and electrification.

Why developers should care: Power availability is becoming a location and deployment variable. Teams choosing cloud regions should consider grid constraints, energy prices and the provider’s ability to secure new capacity, not only GPU instance availability.

Why it ranks here: Editorial assessment 4.0/5. The forecast is not a new AI product, but it quantifies a structural constraint that will shape where and how quickly AI infrastructure can expand.

AI IN CONTEXT

Analysis · not additional reported facts

1. AI is moving from writing code to proving it

AlphaProof Code points to a new workflow: humans define and approve the properties, while agents build the proof and a small trusted kernel checks it. OpenAI’s Control Room addresses the other side of the same problem: when an agent or research job should stop, the enforcement point must sit outside the workload code. Together, they suggest that agent systems will need both stronger evidence of correctness and stronger controls over execution.

2. Power is becoming a first-order compute input

SoftBank’s procurement charge shows what happens when power equipment is reserved before project requirements settle. Infineon’s forecast shows the demand flowing into power semiconductors; CoreWeave’s 4.6GW of contracted power shows how cloud providers are securing sites; and the EIA’s national forecast points to sustained demand growth. These are separate disclosures, but together they make electricity infrastructure part of the AI supply chain.

3. Capital is looping through the AI supply chain

TSMC is investing in two model developers while expanding the capacity they need. Anthropic’s proposed US$26bn private placement includes several strategic technology suppliers, and SoftBank’s results show how financing and equipment reservations can carry risk. This can accelerate capacity planning, but it also means customers, suppliers and investors are increasingly tied to the same demand assumptions.

4. Agent interfaces are becoming work environments

Google’s /swarm and Anthropic’s interactive connectors take agents into repositories and business applications. The next competitive question is less about a polished demo and more about coordination overhead, permissions, verification, auditability and total cost. A useful agent should leave a traceable record of what it changed and why.

DECODE THE JARGON

Plain-English reference
Formal verification
Mathematically proving that a program satisfies a precise specification, rather than relying only on tests or code review.
Lean
A proof assistant and programming language whose small trusted kernel checks whether a mathematical proof follows from its definitions and assumptions.
libsignal
Signal’s reference implementation of its messaging protocol, separate from the full Signal client applications.
AArch64
The 64-bit instruction-set architecture used by many Arm processors.
Machine-code proof
A proof about the compiled instructions that execute, rather than only about source code or an abstract protocol design.
MCP
Model Context Protocol: an open standard for connecting AI systems to external tools, data and applications.
Interactive connector / MCP App
A connected service that can present its own interactive interface inside an AI application, not just return text or structured data.
Fail closed
If a safety or authorisation service is unavailable, deny the operation rather than allow it to continue unchecked.
job_id
A unique identifier attached to a research or agent workload so its model requests can be authorised or revoked centrally.
SiC
Silicon carbide, a wide-bandgap semiconductor material used in efficient high-voltage and high-temperature power electronics.
GaN
Gallium nitride, another wide-bandgap semiconductor material used in high-efficiency power conversion.
300mm wafer
A semiconductor wafer 300 millimetres in diameter. Larger wafers can yield more chips per processing run than 200mm wafers, if the process is industrialised.
Revenue backlog
A company estimate of future revenue tied to customer contracts and remaining performance obligations; it is not cash already received or revenue guaranteed without conditions.
Run-rate revenue
An annualised estimate based on a recent period’s revenue. It is a snapshot, not the same as reported full-year revenue.
PBC
Public benefit corporation: a company form that requires directors to balance shareholder interests with specified public-benefit purposes.
