01CHIPS / COMPUTE3–4 Nov 2026

AMD’s data-centre business nearly doubles as its AI rack strategy moves into production

Record accelerator and server demand is now showing up in the numbers. The less comfortable detail is the price AMD is paying to secure two of its biggest customers.

AMD reported third-quarter revenue of $13.38bn, up 45% year over year, with data-centre revenue reaching a record $8.55bn, up 97%. It forecast roughly $17bn in fourth-quarter revenue and said its Helios rack-scale system, built around MI455X accelerators and Venice server CPUs, is moving into a larger production ramp. CEO Lisa Su told analysts she expects overall revenue growth above 80% in 2027. These are company results and guidance; market-share implications remain to be measured.

A separate Reuters report on 4 November explains a $1.07bn non-cash charge tied to warrants granted to OpenAI and Meta in exchange for chip commitments. The warrants could dilute AMD shareholders if milestones are met. This is an accounting charge, not $1.07bn of operating cash burn. The deals show how chip suppliers are using equity incentives to secure scarce, long-duration demand.

Evidence check: Financial figures are AMD-reported; Reuters adds independent market context and reports the warrant accounting. AMD’s 2027 outlook is guidance, not a realised result.

Why developers should care: The contest is moving from a fast GPU to a complete rack and software stack. Watch MI455X availability, ROCm compatibility, interconnect, memory bandwidth and the work required to move CUDA workloads. Peak silicon specifications alone will not tell you whether a production migration is economical.

Why it ranks here: Top rank for measurable data-centre growth, a real product ramp and direct competitive pressure on Nvidia. The warrant accounting changes the customer economics.

Sources: AMD Q3 results — SEC filing, 3 Nov 2026 · Reuters: AMD forecast and data-centre sales, 3 Nov 2026 · Reuters: $1.07bn warrant charge, 4 Nov 2026
02CHINA / EXPORT CONTROLS3 Nov 2026

Nvidia reportedly asks global cloud partners to keep 51% of services outside China

A new customer-mix rule could close another route by which Chinese companies rent access to advanced US accelerators.

The Financial Times reports that Nvidia has told data-centre partners worldwide to direct at least 51% of their cloud services to customers outside China. The instruction was reportedly delivered verbally by sales and regional teams, applies to future contracts and leaves existing leases untouched. The FT cites five people familiar with the policy; it has not published a written Nvidia rule.

Nvidia’s public response defended the role of US-built data centres and said longstanding US policy encourages American infrastructure to win business worldwide. That statement did not confirm the reported 51% requirement. If enforced, the rule could make overseas GPU leasing harder for Chinese model developers and change the economics of cloud operators in Southeast Asia and other regional hubs.

Evidence check: This is an FT-reported policy, not a confirmed public Nvidia rule or a US government regulation. The 51% figure and scope should be treated as reported, not independently documented.

Why developers should care: Access to accelerators can depend on customer eligibility as well as price and technical fit. It may strengthen incentives to use Huawei Ascend and other domestic systems, but it is not evidence that those systems can yet replace Nvidia at scale. Software portability, HBM supply, interconnect, yields and energy cost still matter.

Why it ranks here: High geopolitical and infrastructure significance, but the policy is based on sources familiar with a verbal instruction rather than a published document.

Sources: Financial Times, 3 Nov 2026 · Times of India summary, 3 Nov 2026
03INDIA / OPEN MODELS3 Nov 2026

IndiaAI Mission releases EKA-1 and two Dhīr models for local and offline deployment

The three releases target Indian-language work, coding and reasoning, plus private deployments where connectivity or data residency is a constraint.

India’s Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology released EKA-1, developed by Soket Labs, and Dhīr 2B and Dhīr 5B, developed by ZenteiQ, under the IndiaAI Mission. The government says EKA-1 has 26.3bn parameters, was trained on about 7.5tn tokens and covers all 22 scheduled Indian languages plus English. It is described as an open, sparse hybrid model for mathematics, code, reasoning and agentic use.

The government describes Dhīr 2B and Dhīr 5B as open-source multilingual assistants for English and 10 Indian languages, with offline-capable, on-premises use for document-grounded automation and knowledge retrieval. The teams received subsidised national AI compute and research support. The release is evidence of a domestic model pipeline, but not yet proof of competitive benchmarks, sustained adoption or production-grade support.

Evidence check: Parameter counts, training volume, language coverage and intended uses come from the government release and company descriptions. Independent benchmark and adoption data were not found in this window.

Why developers should care: Test model-weight access and licence terms, quality across languages and scripts, context limits, quantisation, edge memory requirements, tool calling and inference cost. Air-gapped deployment could matter in banking, government and industry if the models perform reliably on real workloads.

Why it ranks here: High because it adds domestic model supply and an offline deployment option to India’s AI ecosystem. Independent evaluation and clear model cards are the next evidence needed.

Sources: PIB / MeitY release, 3 Nov 2026 · Moneycontrol, 3 Nov 2026 · Mint, 3 Nov 2026
04DATA CENTRES / POWER3 Nov 2026

Drax and Brookfield outline a UK AI campus with more than 1GW of potential capacity

The project pairs powered land, a large grid connection and infrastructure capital. Most of the headline capacity is still a phased ambition.

Drax, Brookfield and its AI infrastructure platform Radiant signed a partnership to develop data-centre capacity at Drax Power Station in North Yorkshire. The partners are targeting more than 1GW across several phases. Phase one is up to 100MW, with commercial operations targeted for the first quarter of 2028; a later phase could add 500MW between 2028 and 2031. Brookfield estimates the full development could attract more than £20bn, including about £2.5bn for the first phase.

Drax says the site has about 4GW of grid access and 2.6GW of active dispatchable generation. Phase one is expected to draw power directly from the grid; later phases may use a behind-the-meter arrangement. Planning, network and regulatory approvals, a suitable tenant, commercial agreements and final investment decisions are still required.

Evidence check: Capacity and investment figures are partner estimates and targets. Reuters independently reports the deal; Drax’s own announcement spells out the conditions and phase timing.

Why developers should care: Power availability, grid queues, cooling, fibre and planning are becoming as important as accelerator supply. A 1GW target is not 1GW of live compute; watch permits, power contracts, tenants and equipment orders.

Why it ranks here: Important infrastructure link between AI capacity and energy assets, but the phased and conditional status prevents treating the headline as delivered capacity.

Sources: Drax, 3 Nov 2026 · Reuters, 3 Nov 2026
05AI SECURITY / AGENTS3 Nov 2026

CrowdStrike is buying agent observability and AI red-team tools — and adding a kill switch

The security vendor is assembling controls for agents before deployment and while they are running.

CrowdStrike signed agreements to acquire Fiddler AI, which monitors and explains agent behaviour, and Dreadnode, which tests models and agents for security weaknesses. The Wall Street Journal reports a combined price of $495m, mostly cash; CrowdStrike’s official announcements do not disclose the purchase price. Fiddler is intended to add pre-deployment evaluation, traceability of decisions and tool calls, and production monitoring. CrowdStrike says it plans to support OpenTelemetry and frameworks including LangGraph, Amazon Bedrock, AWS Strands and Google ADK.

Dreadnode is designed to run more than 100 attack strategies, including jailbreaks, prompt injection and multi-turn agent attacks, against models, applications and MCP servers. Separately, CrowdStrike announced an AI kill switch and an AI Gateway in Falcon Guardian. The gateway is available now; endpoint termination is described as coming later. Acquired Fiddler and Dreadnode capabilities are planned for general availability in Q4 FY27, subject to closing and integration.

Evidence check: Product capabilities and availability dates are CrowdStrike’s statements. The $495m combined price is reported by the WSJ; it is not disclosed in company releases.

Why developers should care: Agent observability is becoming part of the application stack. Teams need traces connecting prompts, tool calls, identities, permissions and resulting actions; they also need repeatable adversarial tests and a way to stop an agent that goes off-task.

Why it ranks here: High because it turns agent security into concrete controls: evaluation, traceability, attack testing and runtime intervention.

Sources: CrowdStrike: Fiddler, 3 Nov 2026 · CrowdStrike: Dreadnode, 3 Nov 2026 · CrowdStrike: Falcon Guardian, 3 Nov 2026 · WSJ: reported $495m price, 3 Nov 2026
06AI FINANCE / INFRASTRUCTURE3 Nov 2026

Meta raises $15bn in bonds as AI infrastructure spending moves deeper into debt markets

The bond sale adds another funding channel to a build-out that is already one of the largest corporate capital programmes in technology.

Meta is raising $15bn through a five-part US-dollar bond offering, according to Reuters and a regulatory filing. The company expects 2026 capital expenditure of $135bn to $145bn, largely tied to AI infrastructure. It previously raised $25bn in April and $30bn last year. Meta’s total debt stood at $83.67bn at the end of September, according to LSEG data cited by Reuters.

The sale is a financing decision, not proof that Meta has exhausted its cash or that the entire amount is earmarked for a single AI project. It shows how quickly compute ambitions are becoming a capital-markets story: data centres, power, networking and accelerators must be funded years before all the capacity earns a return.

Evidence check: Bond amount and capex guidance are reported by Reuters from Meta statements and filings; the longer-term return on spending remains uncertain.

Why developers should care: More infrastructure funding can mean more capacity and cloud supply, but it also raises pressure to improve utilisation and demonstrate returns. Expect scrutiny of inference cost, workload efficiency and product revenue.

Why it ranks here: Important because it quantifies the capital required to scale AI infrastructure and shows debt becoming a recurring part of the funding mix.

Sources: Reuters, 3 Nov 2026
07EDUCATION / PRODUCT POLICY3 Nov 2026

Google’s school AI opt-in comes with a search trade-off

Schools get a clearer administrator switch for AI, but they cannot keep Google Search while disabling all of its AI features.

Google says Gemini, Gemini Notebook, Gemini in Classroom and Google Search will be off for K–12 student accounts until school administrators explicitly enable them. Guided Learning is now globally available in Google Classroom, and Google is building step-by-step tutoring into Search, with a pilot in selected US schools later in November.

There is a catch for districts that want AI disabled: Google says Search and AI are deeply integrated, so turning the products off also removes Search access for student accounts. Google frames the change as giving schools control. Educators and public officials quoted by AP and other outlets argue that the coupling pressures schools to accept AI to retain a basic search tool.

Evidence check: Google’s product changes and rollout plans are company announcements. The coercion criticism is an independent interpretation reported by AP and TechCrunch.

Why developers should care: Admin controls, age settings, data handling and the ability to separate AI-generated answers from ordinary search will shape how schools and public institutions adopt AI.

Why it ranks here: Ranked for its reach across a large education platform and the unusual trade-off between AI controls and access to a core service.

Sources: Google, 3 Nov 2026 · AP, 3 Nov 2026 · TechCrunch, 3 Nov 2026
08MEMORY / SUPPLY CHAIN2–3 Nov 2026

Apple’s margin warning shows the AI memory crunch reaching consumer devices

The data-centre build-out is competing for memory supply that also feeds phones, PCs and other electronics.

Apple reported September-quarter revenue of $114.5bn, up 12% year over year, and a gross margin of 47.9%. CEO John Ternus described current memory pricing as unprecedented and said Apple expected still higher costs in the December quarter and beyond. Reuters reports that Apple is forecasting a 45%–46% gross margin for the December quarter and has increased manufacturing purchase obligations by $33bn to $89.7bn.

Apple has been drawing on memory bought earlier at lower prices while securing additional supply. Reuters links the squeeze to AI data-centre demand absorbing memory capacity. This is broader than HBM alone: the knock-on effect is visible in consumer-electronics component costs.

Evidence check: Apple’s revenue and margin figures are company-reported. The memory-cost outlook and supplier-obligation details are reported by Reuters from the earnings call and filing.

Why developers should care: The same demand wave that fills AI racks can raise costs across the hardware market. Teams building local inference boxes, edge devices or developer workstations should watch memory prices, capacity and upgrade cycles.

Why it ranks here: A cross-stack indicator: a company with exceptional supply-chain leverage is warning that memory pricing is affecting its margins.

Sources: Apple, 2 Nov 2026 · Reuters, 3 Nov 2026
09EUROPE / SOVEREIGN COMPUTE3 Nov 2026

Bull and Schwarz Digits pair European server manufacturing with a sovereign cloud

The French supercomputer maker and the German retail group’s cloud arm want to build a European-controlled AI stack from servers to applications.

Bull and Schwarz Digits signed a memorandum of understanding to develop AI and cloud infrastructure for European organisations, particularly regulated and strategic sectors. Schwarz Digits will bring its STACKIT cloud platform; Bull will contribute AI and high-performance-computing systems and manufacturing at its Angers plant. The partners say they intend to bring BullSequana AI to the STACKIT marketplace and co-design servers using Open Compute Project standards.

Bull also points to direct liquid cooling and rack-scale manufacturing. The announcement does not specify deployment capacity, a financial commitment or a complete accelerator bill of materials. Reuters describes the agreement as preliminary, with no financial terms disclosed. It is a roadmap for a sovereign stack, not yet a new source of operational GPU capacity.

Evidence check: Scope and technical ambitions are partner statements. Reuters independently confirms the preliminary agreement and says financial terms were not disclosed.

Why developers should care: For regulated industries, jurisdiction, data control and local operations can be decisive. The technical test will be competitive accelerators, model tooling, networking, portability and support — not just a European hosting address.

Why it ranks here: A meaningful non-US infrastructure move, but this is an MoU without disclosed scale or financial terms.

Sources: Bull, 3 Nov 2026 · Reuters, 3 Nov 2026
10AI SECURITY / GOVERNANCE3 Nov 2026

Former OpenAI staff say they warned leaders about internal security gaps for years

The Reuters investigation describes concerns about repository access, research discussions and the security review of internal tools.

Five people familiar with the matter told Reuters that some OpenAI employees raised concerns with senior leaders and the board about internal security practices, including broad access to code in a large monorepo, research material shared through GitHub and Slack, and internal sandboxes that were not always reviewed regularly. The concerns predated ChatGPT’s launch, according to the report.

OpenAI told Reuters that its most sensitive work is held separately with strict access controls, that the monorepo does not contain all of its code or model weights, and that it now conducts regular security reviews of internal tools. The reporting describes employee warnings and security-process concerns; it does not establish that every alleged weakness led to a breach.

Evidence check: This is Reuters reporting based on people familiar with the matter, not a formal audit finding. OpenAI’s response is included in the report.

Why developers should care: Least-privilege access, secrets management, sandbox isolation, tool review and audit trails need to cover the agent as well as the human developer.

Why it ranks here: High-impact security reporting because agents can find and exploit internal access mistakes at a different speed. Allegations and company response are kept distinct.

Sources: Reuters, 3 Nov 2026

AI IN CONTEXT

Analysis, not additional reporting

1. Chip competition is a systems and financing contest

AMD’s results show demand for an alternative to Nvidia, but the warrants show how suppliers are paying to secure customers. The test is whether racks ship, software ports cleanly and production workloads run at an acceptable cost.

2. China compute is shifting from ownership to access

If the FT-reported Nvidia rule is enforced, overseas cloud leases may become harder for Chinese customers. Domestic accelerators need chips, HBM, packaging, networking, compiler support and reliable power — not just a roadmap.

3. Power and memory are now front-page constraints

Drax’s phased campus depends on grid access, permits and tenants. Apple’s warning shows the same demand wave reaching consumer electronics. Compute capacity is constrained by the whole supply chain.

4. Agent adoption is pulling controls into the developer stack

CrowdStrike is packaging tracing, adversarial testing and runtime intervention. OpenAI’s security reporting points to the same issue: identity, permissions, logs and sandbox boundaries need to be designed in.

DECODE THE JARGON

Plain-English reference
HBM
High-bandwidth memory stacked close to accelerators to feed them data quickly.
Rack-scale
A tightly integrated rack of accelerators, CPUs, networking, power and cooling.
ROCm
AMD’s software platform for programming and running workloads on its GPUs.
Warrant
A contract giving its holder the right to buy shares later, often after milestones.
Air-gapped
A computer or network disconnected from the public internet and external networks.
Agent observability
Tracing what an agent was asked to do, which tools it called and what it changed.
MCP
Model Context Protocol, a common way for AI apps to connect models to tools and data.
MoU
Memorandum of understanding: a statement of intent, usually short of a final contract.
World model
An AI system that predicts or simulates how an environment changes.
Token credits
Prepaid model usage units, subject to provider accounting and expiry terms.
Behind-the-meter
Power supplied on the customer side of the grid connection, often from on-site generation.
Sovereign AI
AI infrastructure, data and operations under a country’s or region’s legal control.
